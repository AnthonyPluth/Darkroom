# Re-upload large Google Photos videos from a first-generation Pixel

Draft for [#591](https://github.com/AnthonyPluth/Darkroom/issues/591). The owner wants to recover Google account storage by backing up selected videos from a first-generation Pixel at original quality. This proposal needs owner approval and disposable tests before it can be used with household media. Google documents the first-generation Pixel's Original quality benefit. Whether an imported video in this Pixel's Syncthing folder receives it needs a live check. [Darkroom spec](../SPEC.md) · [Pixel backup quality](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/6220791?hl=en)

## Find and approve one video

Start with one person and one Google account. The owner opens Google One's Photos storage manager for that account and identifies the large videos that count toward its quota. Darkroom proposes standalone videos with an exact source association to that person and account, sorted by original byte size, largest first. Use a per-person minimum of 1 GiB by default, subject to owner approval. Eligibility compares the unedited original's bytes to the threshold. Google's displayed storage estimate describes quota usage. The owner confirms the old item consumes storage, even if they found it through Takeout. Exclude unsupported formats, anything above Google's video backup limit of 10 GB, and anything the Pixel cannot hold within its configured cap and free space. [Google storage manager](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/6374270?hl=en) · [Google Photos upload limits](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6174018?hl=en)

Use a completed Takeout's exact-byte provenance when it can be tied to the selected cloud item. If it cannot, the owner must download the selected item with Google's own download control and use the resulting size and SHA-1 to establish the match. A filename, visual resemblance, capture date, automatic tier 2 match, or an older export on its own cannot identify the item. The owner confirms the account and the individual item's locator in Google Photos. Require a unique same-person match to a managed original. Hold ambiguous identities, shared or partner items, Live Photo or motion-photo components, edited representations, pending review or unresolved metadata, and missing originals. Candidate discovery is read-only.

The owner approves one item and one destination Pixel at a time. A receipt binds the person, Google account, old item locator, Immich owner and asset IDs, managed version, SHA-1, size, destination Pixel/device, and approval time. The owner's approval covers staging that video. Moving it to Trash is a separate confirmation after the checks below. A destination, account, ownership, item identity or byte change invalidates the approval. A shared Pixel must serve this person and back up to this exact account. Reserve at most one active video per account and per Pixel so retries and simultaneous approvals cannot stage competing copies.

Before approval, show the video's current album or sharing context where the owner can inspect it. Trash removes references from albums and shares. A re-upload can get a new item identity and lose album membership, comments, sharing links, edits or Google-only details even when the bytes match. Exclude items with contexts the owner needs to preserve from this first workflow. [Google Photos deletion effects](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128858?hl=en)

## Proof before any Google Photos removal

All checks must pass when Darkroom presents the owner with the next manual removal step. Recheck after any change or interruption and separately before permanently removing an item from Trash. Present the result for the current session and item only. The owner then checks the active Google account and target again in the Google Photos web app. Darkroom cannot lock either service across that human step. Record that boundary separately from the acceptance for managed inbox cleanup.

1. **Immich custody.** With the person's owner-bound managed reader, check the current asset's owner, managed library (`libraryId: null`), online and untrashed state, checksum and size. Stream the unedited original and verify SHA-1 and size against the selected Google original. Require a committed managed receipt or verified asset version. A thumbnail, transcoded stream, external-library record, same-shot hold or file under `originals/` or `google-only/` is insufficient. The relevant mapping comes from the managed import or backfill work. If it is absent, leave the item in Google Photos. [Managed storage design](immich-managed-storage.md) · [Managed storage epic #530](https://github.com/AnthonyPluth/Darkroom/issues/530)
2. **Recovery point.** Require the owner's current restore-rehearsal record, the acceptance for this workflow, and the custody and coverage predicates of the #545 gate. The latest completed attempt succeeded, no open attempt is overdue, and a succeeded point is at most 14 days old, still retained, and covers Immich database and media plus Darkroom database and secret key. Its manifest contains this exact owner, asset ID and SHA-1, verified before the point began. Reuse the backup proof after #545 lands. The inbox release clock and its per-part or zip proof apply to inbox cleanup. A Google Photos receipt grants no inbox release authority. [Recovery-point gate](recovery-point-gate.md)
3. **Pixel copy.** Check a current, scoped delivery receipt. The exact approved bytes must remain intact in Darkroom's Send Only folder, and the connected peer must report fully synced after Syncthing scanned this copy. The owner also confirms the on-device file and destination in the same check. Before permanent removal, verify the Pixel copy again, even if it was present before moving the old item to Trash. No cached sync indicator or owner confirmation can substitute for the other evidence.

The checks are preconditions for the owner to proceed. Record their timestamp and the recovery point ID against the current receipt. On a stale read, timeout, failed backup, detached peer, replaced file, unknown cloud state or unavailable destination, show the reason and stop. If the video is already in Trash, keep the copies and tell the owner to check whether the old item can still be restored.

## Stage on the Pixel and verify the replacement

Build on `pixel_folder.place_managed` and `pixel_check.intact` for bounded, checked staging from the unedited Immich original. Keep the file separate from ordinary backup traffic in the existing per-person subtree of `Photos.pixel_root`. Use an approved, one-video staging area the owner can verify is excluded from automatic backup until the intended upload step. The disposable test must confirm how the Pixel recognizes that area, including the effect of backing up all device folders. Reuse the Send Only path checks, caps, atomic placement and byte verification. Never transcode, edit metadata or place an XMP sidecar. Reject a path collision without overwriting the existing file.

`pixel_source.returns_home` currently stops Takeout-only assets from returning to their source Google account. Preserve that rule for ordinary queueing and requeueing. Add one narrow placement path for an active owner-approved receipt, bound to the exact copy generation, original bytes, person, account and device. This path gives no authority to schedule another asset, change the peer, broaden Google backup, or call the ordinary losing-copy remover. Keep the staged copy pinned against routine Pixel pruning. An earlier Takeout match can still describe the charged copy or an older generation.

The reports in `transport.read_reports` describe a folder and device. For this workflow require a fresh report from the approved peer, generated after a scan that indexes this relative path and generation. Require 100% completion with zero needed items and deletes, then recheck the staged file's digest and size. A reconnect, folder change or changed file generation invalidates the evidence until refreshed. The owner checks that the file is present on the Pixel, can be opened, and corresponds to the staged file (check size and digest on-device if necessary to resolve identity). If the pinned Syncthing version cannot bind completion to the indexed file, the item stays blocked. [Syncthing API spike](../spikes/syncthing-api.md)

The owner initiates the one upload on the Pixel with the account and Original quality settings verified, using the device steps established by the disposable test. Upload proof combines a per-item backup check on the Pixel with confirmation of a distinct replacement visible in the same account from a separate browser. Observe the old item and replacement separately, with the old one still in Trash if it was moved there first. Confirm the new item's Original quality and download its unedited bytes for a SHA-1 and size match. Record the old and replacement locators and the observation times in the receipt. Mark ambiguous or unavailable identity, quality or bytes as awaiting proof. A Takeout snapshot started after the observed replacement can corroborate the bytes. A matching hash alone in Takeout, a local thumbnail or an app-wide “Backup complete” message cannot distinguish the old object from the replacement. [Per-item backup status](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6193313?hl=en)

The receipt records each transition and can resume after interruption by asking the owner to reconcile what they see on Google Photos and the Pixel. Resume never repeats a cloud action or infers success from an interrupted confirmation. Retire staging through the existing Pixel transient-copy removal boundary only after the replacement has been verified, storage accounting has settled, and an automatic exact-byte Takeout match for the destination account is tied to the replacement after its upload. If Takeout cannot distinguish the replacement, keep the copy and report why. On cancellation, retain any staged copy until the original is verified live again and the existing exact-byte pruning proof holds for the current copy, or the owner approves a later design for disposal. No time-based pruning or new remover is needed here.

## Order, Trash and undo

The owner performs each cloud action by hand in Google Photos on the web. The Takeout browser worker continues to download exports. It gets no Photos mutation job, selector, authenticated session handoff, or access to the approval list. The Library API has no media deletion endpoint. The approval list is the owner's checklist for a single exact item. If the account or locator cannot be verified, or Google offers only a permanent action when the expected action is Trash, stop. No bulk selection or empty-all Trash action is part of this design. [Google Photos Library API](https://developers.google.com/photos/library/reference/rest)

There is no established order for this setup yet. Try upload-first in the disposable account. If a distinct uncharged item can coexist with the charged one, verify the replacement and then move only the charged item to Trash. If Google deduplicates while the old item is live, the next candidate order is to stage and sync first, pause backup on devices whose copies need to survive, move the old item to Trash from the web, recheck the Pixel copy, then initiate the verified Pixel upload with the old item still in Trash. Google's help says resuming backup can re-upload the item or remove its local copy. If the test cannot show the entire sequence works with the old item restorable, there is no production path under this design. A test of permanent removal before re-upload belongs only on disposable media. A purge-first result would need a new owner decision and design review. [Google Photos deletion and device backup](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128858?hl=en)

Undo means restoring the exact old item from Google Photos Trash while it is still there. #591 refers to a 60-day window. Google's current documentation says 30 days. Use the item's actual expiry if Google shows it, with the documented 30 days as the outer bound for reminders, and confirm the behaviour in the test account. After expiry or permanent deletion, Immich and the recovery point can provide the original bytes, but cannot restore the old Google item or its links. If proof of the replacement is still missing near expiry, alert the owner to restore the old item while it remains available. [Google Photos restore policy](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/9343482?hl=en)

After replacement proof, wait for Trash expiry by default. If the owner wants faster quota relief and the experiment proves the necessary controls exist, the owner may permanently remove just the verified old item from Trash, with separate confirmation and fresh proof. The owner checks the Photos storage subtotal in Google One before staging, after the Trash move, and after expiry or removal and re-upload have settled. Google says permanently clearing Trash frees quota and accounting can take up to 72 hours after bulk removal. Check that the new item's details report the expected quality and quota treatment where available, and that the Photos quota released by the old item stays available after the replacement. Isolate the measurement from other uploads and deletions. If rounding or other activity obscures the difference, or the replacement counts toward quota, show “Storage savings unconfirmed” even when the bytes are verified. [Google One storage accounting](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/6374270?hl=en) · [Pixel backup quality](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/6220791?hl=en)

Each selected video shows one current state and the evidence and time behind it. The sequence is approved, staged, synced, ready for the owner's web action or Pixel upload, old item in Trash, replacement verified, and storage reclaimed or unconfirmed. ## Follow-up issues and checks

This work touches promises 1 and 2 for original-byte preservation, one-way Pixel delivery, transient-copy pruning and the separate Google deletion boundary. Promise 4 keeps account sign-in with the owner, promise 5 keeps media bytes unchanged, and promise 6 governs the UI. Keep the existing module and path restrictions and add a structural check that the one exceptional same-account placement entry point requires an approved receipt. Keep a deny-by-default check for Google Photos mutation through the worker and Immich delete, trash, purge or replacement through its provider. A negative test must exercise every gate refusal, including one changed condition at a time, and verify it leaves the existing copies intact.

## Open questions

1. Is a 1 GiB per-person minimum and one video per approval the right starting point? Which person, account and first-generation Pixel should the owner use for the eventual pilot?
2. Does the owner want to exclude every item with album or sharing references in the first release, including items whose old album memberships they could recreate manually?
3. Which order passes V2–V5, what can verify the indexed file's sync state, and which approved step preserves local copies when backup is resumed? If the tests only succeed with permanent removal first, stop and return to #591 for a design decision.
4. After a verified replacement, should the first release offer individual early permanent removal or only wait for Trash expiry? What lead time should the owner get for a restore reminder, and what is the approved plan for retained staging when replacement proof stays inconclusive?
5. Where should the owner see the review list and status, and what wording should appear on the signed-off boards? Confirm the owner access check, especially on an installation that permits sessions without sign-in.
